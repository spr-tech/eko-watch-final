How it works

From one resident's report to a street the whole ward can see is missed.

  1. 1

    A resident reports

    A blocked drain on their street, with a photo where they can and a short note.

  2. 2

    A neighbour confirms

    Each person can confirm a report once. Reports with more confirmations rank higher.

  3. 3

    The ward office sees it

    On its dashboard, next to missed pickups and roads that are flooded now.

  4. 4

    The operator records pickups

    After collecting, they mark the street done. Once per street, per day.

  5. 5

    No record means missed

    If a collection day passes with nothing recorded, the street shows as missed. Nobody has to report it.

Four things you can report

Pick the one that matches what you see. A photo helps, but a short note is enough.

Missed pickup

Collection day came and went and nobody came. EkoWatch also flags it on its own when no pickup was recorded.

Illegal dumping

Refuse left on a corner, a verge or an empty plot.

Blocked drain

A drain choked with refuse or silt, ideally reported before the rain turns it into a flood.

Flooded road

Shows as current for 6 hours, then expires unless a neighbour confirms it again.

Refuse that isn't collected ends up in drains. Blocked drains flood roads.

That's why EkoWatch keeps both on one record for each street, so the ward can see one problem leading to the next.

Three roles, three views

Your role comes with your account. Nobody picks one on screen.

Residents

Report problems, confirm what neighbours report, and check the collection days for their street.

Waste operators

See only the streets assigned to them, and mark each one done after collection.

The ward office

Sees missed pickups by operator, blocked drains and current floods across the whole ward.

Rules built into EkoWatch

  • Your name never appears on a report.
  • Location data is removed from photos before they're stored.
  • Only the ward office sees how each operator is performing.
  • Every list tells you how old its newest report is.

EkoWatch is a community record for one ward. It doesn't forecast floods, send out crews or replace any agency.

A web page can't prove a photo was taken on the spot rather than picked from a gallery, so every photo is stamped with the time it reached us.
